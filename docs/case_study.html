1Executive Summary

Sunshine's merchandising and accounts teams managed supplier advance deposits — partial payments made to suppliers before goods are shipped — through a single shared Excel workbook. As transaction volume grew past several hundred live records, the spreadsheet became the bottleneck for the entire payment operation: no access control, no approval workflow, no audit trail, manual analytics, and no protection against overwriting a colleague's edits.

The Advance Deposit Tracker replaces that workbook with a production web platform: a role-based dashboard system for five distinct user roles, a public submission form for merchandisers in the field, an enforced status workflow with a Head-of-Merchandiser approval gate for risky suppliers, automated shipment-risk analytics, one-click report exports, a complete audit trail, and an AI-powered business-intelligence assistant.

Capability highlights

  • A financial control tower, not a tracker. Every advance is followed from intake to shipment with live risk classification, cost-of-fund exposure computed to the day, and a Non-Performing-Assets panel for leadership.
  • Risk that polices itself. Suppliers who miss their grace window are flagged automatically; every future advance to them is intercepted and routed through Head-of-Merchandiser sign-off — no human has to remember who defaulted.
  • Money movements are irreversible on purpose. Processing a payment locks the record at the database level itself — the platform refuses post-payment tampering, even from the API.
  • Client-defined mathematics, guaranteed by tests. The Cost-of-Fund formula was specified by the business, implemented to the letter, and its worked example is encoded as an automated test — a future code change that alters the number fails the build.
  • Ask questions in plain English. An AI business-intelligence assistant converts natural-language questions into safe, read-only queries and explains the answer.
  • Field intake without friction. A public form lets merchandisers submit from anywhere with no login; submissions bind to their registered account automatically and obey every risk gate.
  • Governance as configuration. Which role sees which field, which role opens which analytics section, grace days, interest rates — all admin-editable settings, not code changes.
  • Engineered for distance. Compute co-located with the database region, round trips halved, responses compressed, actions acknowledged instantly — sub-second interactions measured from India.
  • Fully documented for the archive. This case study plus a companion Formula & Calculation Reference put every rule and every formula on paper for client sign-off.

2Background — The Excel Problem

The original process ran on one workbook (the "Deposit Sunshine Tracker"), passed between merchandisers, the accounts team, and finance. Every weakness of spreadsheet-as-database applied:

Pain pointConsequence in daily operations
No access controlAnyone with the file could see or edit every column — deposit amounts, bank references, supplier risk notes — regardless of their role.
No workflow"Approved", "on hold", "cancelled" were free-text cell values. Nothing prevented a payment being processed for a request that was never approved.
No audit trailWhen a number changed, nobody could say who changed it, when, or what the previous value was.
Concurrent-edit conflictsTwo people editing at once meant lost updates and emailed copies named tracker_FINAL_v3 (2).xlsx.
Manual analyticsOverdue-shipment tracking, cost-of-fund exposure, and supplier default risk were recomputed by hand with formulas that silently broke as rows were added.
No supplier risk gateNothing stopped a new advance being paid to a supplier who had already defaulted on a previous shipment.
Free-format dataDates, currencies, and percentages were typed inconsistently — 530+ variations of payment terms existed for what were really ~33 distinct terms.
The core requirement distilled from stakeholder sessions: "the right people should be able to do only the right things, every change must be traceable, risky suppliers must require sign-off, and the numbers we look at must compute themselves."

3Solution Overview

The platform delivers four connected capabilities:

3.1 Controlled intake

  • In-app request form for merchandisers with live validation, supplier auto-fill (fixed deposit amounts), and a curated 33-term payment-terms catalogue.
  • Public submission form (no login required) so field merchandisers can submit against their registered e-mail; submissions bind automatically to their account.
  • Flagged-supplier interception: selecting a defaulted supplier triggers an inline warning; the submitter may withdraw or proceed — proceeding routes the request to the Head of Merchandiser for explicit approval before it can ever reach the payment queue.

3.2 Enforced workflow

  • Nine request statuses and thirteen legal transitions (hold, resume, cancel, reopen, approve, reject, process) — each restricted to specific roles and enforced in the API, in the database itself, and in the UI.
  • Payment processing locks the record; locked records are read-only for everyone below Super Admin.

3.3 Automated analytics

  • Per-request analytics snapshots: grace ETD, overdue days, payment-to-ship days, cost-of-fund exposure, and an on-time / delayed / defaulted risk classification — recomputed automatically on every relevant change and on a 30-minute scheduler.
  • Suppliers are auto-flagged when any of their shipments exceeds the ETD grace period.
  • Dashboards: KPI tiles, delay buckets, month-on-month / year-to-date trend charts, drill-downs by merchandiser / vertical / customer, and a Non-Performing-Assets panel.

3.4 Governance

  • Field-level audit log (who, what, old value, new value, when) for every mutation.
  • Role-scoped report exports (Excel / CSV / PDF, 18 columns).
  • Admin console: user management, payment-terms catalogue, per-role field visibility, per-role analytics section permissions, system configuration.
  • An AI BI assistant that translates plain-English questions into read-only SQL and summarises the result.

4System Architecture

The system is a three-tier architecture deployed on Railway with Supabase providing PostgreSQL, authentication, and row-level security. Both application services run in Railway's Singapore region, co-located as closely as possible with the database in AWS Mumbai and with the India-based user base.

4.1 Design decisions worth recording

  • Transaction-pooled Postgres: the API connects through pgbouncer transaction pooling; prepared-statement caching is disabled and statement names randomised — a hard requirement discovered in production (SAVEPOINTs and session-level advisory locks are also unusable through this pooler).
  • Defence in depth for workflow: the status machine is enforced three times — Python rules (role-aware), a PostgreSQL trigger (last line of defence, role-blind), and UI gating. A write that bypasses the API still cannot make an illegal transition.
  • Single-owner scheduler: with two uvicorn workers, a file-lock election ensures exactly one APScheduler instance runs — previously both workers ran the 30-minute analytics refresh, doubling database load.
  • Post-response computation: analytics snapshot seeding runs as a background task after the HTTP response is sent, keeping user-facing writes fast while analytics stay near-real-time.

7Request Lifecycle & Workflow Engine

7.1 Triple enforcement

LayerMechanism
Application (Python)status_transitions.py — a map of (from, to) → allowed roles; raises a domain error with a human-readable message before any write.
Database (PostgreSQL)enforce_status_transition() trigger — rejects any UPDATE making an illegal transition, even from a rogue client with direct DB access.
UI (Next.js)Buttons render only for legal actions in the current status for the current role; in-flight actions disable all controls to prevent double-fires.
Lesson learned: when the HoM statuses were added, the Python rules were updated but the database trigger initially was not — approvals failed at the last line of defence. The fix (and the convention now recorded in the runbook) is that the three layers ship together in one migration.

10Performance Engineering

Mid-programme, users reported multi-second waits on submits and page loads. A three-track audit (backend hot paths, frontend data-fetching, infrastructure) traced the slowness to four compounding causes — and fixed all of them in one coordinated release.

10.1 Root cause #1 — geography

The database lives in AWS Mumbai; the application originally ran in Railway's US-East (Virginia) region, with users in India. Every database query paid a ~230 ms planet-crossing round trip — and the submit flow made ~20 of them. The application was relocated to Railway's Singapore region (~60 ms to Mumbai), collapsing the dominant cost of every operation.

10.2 Root cause #2 — database round-trip bloat

FixEffect
Three-tier record loading: scalar-only fetch for validation, 4-relation fetch for mutation responses, full 9-relation fetch only for detail viewsSubmit reload: 10 queries → 5; payment paths: −9 queries each
Server-generated defaults returned via RETURNING (eager defaults) instead of a follow-up refresh query−1 query per write, everywhere; also eliminated a whole class of async lazy-load crashes
Analytics snapshot seeding moved to a post-response background task−2 to −4 queries off the user-facing critical path
Connection liveness ping disabled (redundant behind pgbouncer)−1 query on every single request
Five new hot-path indexes (action tables, list filters, submitter e-mail)Seq-scans → index scans on the busiest lookups

10.3 Root cause #3 — frontend overhead

  • Auth check per navigation was a network call to the auth provider; replaced with a local cookie/JWT check — 100–400 ms saved on every page change.
  • Refetch storms: every button click invalidated the entire request cache (6+ queries refired). Invalidation is now scoped to exactly the affected list and record.
  • Optimistic updates: approve, reject, hold, resume, cancel, reopen and process-payment flip the UI instantly and reconcile with the server in the background — perceived latency for these actions is now effectively zero.
  • Bundle diet: the AI chat (and its markdown renderer) is lazy-loaded; dev tooling excluded from production builds.

10.4 Root cause #4 — background waste

  • Both server workers ran their own scheduler, refreshing all ~600 analytics snapshots twice every 30 minutes with one commit per row. Now: a single elected scheduler, finished/cancelled records skipped, and commits batched 100-at-a-time.
  • Responses gzip-compressed (large list payloads shrink ~80%+); reference data served with 5-minute HTTP caching.

11Reliability & Error Handling

11.1 The "phantom CORS error" — and its permanent fix

Early in production, every backend crash surfaced in browsers as a misleading "blocked by CORS policy" network error, hiding the real cause. The root issue was architectural: the framework's default catch-all exception handler sits outside the CORS middleware, so error responses lost their CORS headers and browsers refused to read them.

Permanent fix: a custom error-envelope middleware placed inside the CORS layer guarantees that any unhandled exception returns structured JSON with CORS headers. Users now see the actual error message; no failure can masquerade as a network problem again.

11.2 Other hardening shipped

  • Business-rule violations raised by database triggers are translated into clean, human-readable 400 responses (the trigger's own message) instead of blank 500s.
  • Manually-constructed validation errors anywhere in the codebase are globally mapped to proper 422 responses.
  • Malformed date filters on analytics and reports no longer crash — they are dropped gracefully.
  • A full role-flow audit verified every button every role can see maps to an action the backend permits — eliminating dead buttons, stranded records (e.g. reopened requests with no path back to the queue), and broken landing redirects.
  • Structured JSON logging throughout; deploy history and operational runbook maintained per release.

12Analytics & Business Intelligence

12.1 The analytics engine

Every request carries a computed snapshot: grace ETD (estimated departure + configurable grace days), ETD-grace overdue days, payment-to-ship days, payment-to-request days, cost-of-fund exposure (configurable annual rate applied beyond a grace window), and a derived risk status (on-time / delayed / defaulted). Snapshots refresh on every relevant write (post-response) and via the 30-minute scheduler; grace days and rates are Super-Admin configuration, not code.

12.2 Dashboards

  • KPI tiles (overdue counts, exposure totals) with permission-gated sections per role.
  • Delay buckets, breakdowns by merchandiser / vertical / customer, cross-filtered drill-down tables, per-supplier analytics pages.
  • Month-on-month and year-to-date trend charts with a year selector.
  • Non-Performing-Assets panel: overdue suppliers (auto/manual flag provenance, max overdue days) and merchandiser performance.

12.3 AI assistant

A chat panel available on every dashboard translates questions like "which suppliers have the highest overdue exposure this quarter?" into read-only SQL against the live schema, executes it with an enforced row cap, and returns a natural-language summary with the underlying data. Access is role-gated and every AI query is audit-logged.

15Appendix B — Glossary
